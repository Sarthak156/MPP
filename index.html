01 · Introduction

What is Autonomous-Vehicle Perception?

Autonomous vehicles must continuously understand their surroundings to operate safely. A perception system processes information from sensors such as cameras and converts it into meaningful information about the environment.

Our proposed system aims to
Analyze road scenes using visual input
Detect important objects around the vehicle
Track objects across consecutive frames
Understand position & movement of surroundings
Identify objects relevant to the vehicle’s path
Support safety-related decision making
Key idea — the goal is not just to detect objects, but to understand the surrounding driving environment.

02 · Problem Statement

Raw images are not enough

A camera provides a continuous stream of images — but raw pixels do not tell an autonomous vehicle what actions may be required. Simply detecting an object is not enough. The system may need to determine:

What object is present?
Where is it located?
How is it moving?
How close is it to the vehicle?
Is it in the driving region?
Could it create collision risk?
Object detection, tracking, spatial understanding and safety analysis are often treated as separate problems.

05 · Proposed System

System architecture

● Input
Recorded driving video or live camera feed, processed frame by frame.
● Output
Detected & tracked objects + environmental information + potential safety / risk information.
Parallel analysis

Position, distance and movement are analyzed as parallel branches of the same perception layer.

Modular design

Each component can be improved or replaced independently — model, tracker, estimators.

Real-time path

The complete pipeline is designed for low-latency, frame-by-frame operation.

06 · Methodology

How the system will work

1

STEPCapture

Obtain frames from a camera or recorded driving video.

2

STEPDetect

An AI object-detection model identifies road objects and their locations.

3

STEPTrack

A multi-object tracker associates objects across consecutive frames.

4

STEPAnalyze

Object position · movement · driving-region relevance · approximate distance.

5

STEPTemporal analysis

Information across frames studies motion and can estimate Time-to-Collision (TTC).

6

STEPRisk assessment

Available information is combined to classify the level of collision risk.

7

STEPVisualization

A real-time interface displays the perceived environment and safety information.

09 · Conclusion

From detection to understanding

Autonomous vehicles need more than simple object recognition — they must understand objects, positions, movement, and their relationship with the vehicle’s path. Our project begins with visual object detection and progressively grows into a broader real-time perception system.
